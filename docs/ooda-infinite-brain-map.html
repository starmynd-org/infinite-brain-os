OODA, the Real Loop, and the Infinite Brain

John Boyd never drew a four-box cycle. He drew a web where Orient dominates and action usually flows straight from orientation. This page shows the real loop, then maps it onto the Infinite Brain OS, and marks honestly where the architecture is strong and where the web is still broken.

The correction

The four-box cartoon is the misread, not the model

Most people teach OODA as a clockwise pipeline: observe, then orient, then decide, then act, repeat, win by going faster. Boyd's own corpus calls this the single most damaging misread. The fix is not to wire four boxes faster. It is to recognize that Orient is the generative center and that most action bypasses Decide entirely.

What people draw

Four equal boxes in a circle. Every action passes through Decide. The win condition is tempo: cycle faster than the other side.

Observe → Orient → Decide → Act → repeat

What Boyd drew (1995)

A web with more arrows running backward and sideways than forward. Orient is the largest box, the only one with internal structure, wired to every other box. Decide is optional and usually skipped. The win condition is a better-matched orientation, not raw speed.

How it runs

Two circuits, and the ladder as a novelty selector

Boyd says two circuits must run at once: a fast implicit one that deploys the existing repertoire, and a slow explicit one that builds new repertoire for genuine novelty. The brain maps both cleanly.

CircuitOODA pathwayIn the brainStatus
Implicit (fast)Orient to Act, bypass Decidebasic task, workflow, fat skill, learned auto-handle rulethrottled off at L1
Explicit (slow)Orient to Decide to Actscoper, then swarm-ai, sprint, or projectrunning, human-gated
The refinement this surfaces. Today the planning ladder (task, swarm, sprint, project) is chosen by size and effort. Boyd's frame says it should be chosen by novelty: does the existing repertoire cover this work, or must new orientation be built. Routing by novelty rather than size is a small change to the scoper, and a more faithful selector.
What to build next

The missing half: the Act-to-Orient feedback plane

The brain has rich feed-forward and almost no feedback. In Boyd's terms that is not a smaller loop, it is a broken one. Building the feedback plane is the highest-leverage OODA work in the system, above more autonomy or more departments, and the ratified design for it is the wager ledger in the next section. One build closes all three weak points of the diagnostic test at once.

Restores interaction

Results, outcomes, and mismatch flow back. The system can tell whether it is winning.

Triggers novelty

A mismatch signal flags when an orientation has gone stale and re-orientation is due.

Completes the web

Feed-forward plus feedback is a loop. Feed-forward alone is a pipeline.

Mismatch is fuel, not failure. The plane records expectation against outcome and surfaces the gap. That gap is the raw material of re-orientation, and the sensor a cascade-containment quarantine reads from. Spec: knowledge/ai-architecture/synthesis/feedback-plane-act-to-orient-loop.md.
The build (ratified)

The wager ledger: bookkeeping for AI harnesses

This is how the feedback plane is built. Every consequential item is booked as a scientific process: a pre-registered, falsifiable prediction tied to a metric, scored later against an exogenous result and diagnosed. The brain becomes the durable hypothesis, action, and result ledger that sits under any harness, so you own the learning when the harness changes.

observation→ routing→ disposition→ wager→ action→ verdict→ improvement
Green steps are the new records. Every other stage reuses an existing brain contract.

Three new tables, everything else reused

new

wager

The pre-registered, falsifiable prediction: a metric, a direction or threshold, a horizon, a confidence. Immutable once written, which makes pre-registration nearly free in git.

new

wager_verdict

The result scored against an exogenous source, the surprise (mismatch), and a hard-to-vary diagnosis that can raise a re-orientation candidate.

new

component_touch

Which rule, skill, agent, or canon node shaped the action, and which Orient sub-element it played. The attribution edge that powers component health.

Reused, not rebuilt: intake record, routing decision, processed receipt, pkm-opportunity, the result envelope, the nine signals, the metric primitive, and the planning ladder.

The four rules that keep it honest

Falsifiable Pre-registered, immutable Measured, not asserted Hard-to-vary diagnosis

Headline metric: calibration, is 0.8 confidence right 80 percent of the time. A periodic scientist agent reviews verdicts and recommends architecture changes, each of which is itself a pre-registered wager. It recommends; it never self-approves canon.

Where it lives. Git holds the contracts, the immutable wagers, the touch-edges, and the diagnoses. The analytical plane holds the fact tables, the scores, and the calibration view, and it defaults to a local Postgres (swappable to a managed BI platform later, with no fork of truth). Component scores never live in canon frontmatter. Operative contract: _system/wager-ledger-rules.md.
